### 8. Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA)

We do not currently collect biometric identifiers or biometric information as defined under the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act, 740 ILCS 14 ("BIPA"). This includes:

- Retina or iris scans
- Fingerprints
- Voiceprints
- Scans of hand or face geometry

If we begin to collect such information in the future (for example, in connection with practitioner identity verification or patient outcome tracking), we will:

- Provide written notice to data subjects
- Obtain written consent before collection
- Disclose the purpose and duration of collection, storage, and use
- Maintain a publicly available retention and destruction schedule
- Implement reasonable safeguards

`[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED — BIPA is one of the most strictly enforced privacy statutes in the U.S. with statutory damages of $1,000-$5,000 per violation. If any biometric data is collected (including potentially through future portal features, treatment outcome tracking with photographs, or device authentication), full BIPA compliance is required and a separate written BIPA notice and consent flow must be implemented.]`

### 9. Health Information and HIPAA

However, when practitioners use The Precise System and the practitioner portal in clinical settings:

- Patient data logged through the practitioner portal is treated as Protected Health Information (PHI)
- All such data is de-identified at the point of capture before flowing into the Data Intelligence Layer
- The Company implements administrative, physical, and technical safeguards consistent with HIPAA Security Rule standards
- Practitioner-facing portal terms include a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) where applicable

`[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED — when practitioner portal launches with patient data logging, full HIPAA assessment required: Privacy Rule, Security Rule, Breach Notification Rule. Determine BAA status with each subprocessor handling PHI. Most consumer marketing site activity does not trigger HIPAA, but the portal will.]`

## Pages to create

- `/privacy` — Privacy Policy page

## Visual treatment

Both pages should use the same document-style register as `/about`:
- Single column, max-w-[680px]
- Bone-100 background, ink text
- Numbered sections, no decorative imagery
- Eyebrow at top: `§ TERMS OF SERVICE` or `§ PRIVACY POLICY`
- Display heading in Fraunces
- Body in Inter 17-19px, line-height 1.7
- Section headings in Fraunces 24-28px
- Effective Date and Last Updated displayed prominently near top

## Footer integration

Add `/terms` and `/privacy` links to the site footer in the legal/policies section. Most B2B sites cluster these as "Terms · Privacy · Cookies" — small text, ink-500 color, hover ink-700.

## Cookie consent

If EU traffic is expected at all, implement a cookie consent banner that:
- Appears on first visit
- Provides clear opt-in/opt-out for non-essential cookies
- Logs consent decisions for compliance
- Allows users to change preferences later

PostHog has a built-in consent feature. Other options include Termly, OneTrust, or a custom implementation.

## Required attorney review topics

Before publishing on the live site, an Illinois-licensed attorney with privacy and medical device experience should specifically review:

1. **BIPA exposure** — confirm no current biometric collection; advise on portal/treatment-tracking features that may trigger BIPA
2. **HIPAA scope** — confirm Site does not currently trigger HIPAA; plan for portal launch
3. **FDA disclosure language** — calibrate exactly to current 510(k) status
4. **State-by-state compliance** — confirm marketing and selling laser devices across all U.S. states
5. **GDPR/UK GDPR** — implement DPA with subprocessors, transfer mechanisms, DSAR workflow
6. **Indemnification and liability caps** — verify enforceability under Illinois law
7. **Arbitration clause** — decide whether to add arbitration; consider class action waiver
8. **Cookie consent** — ensure compliance with applicable jurisdictions
9. **Data retention** — align periods with medical device statute of limitations
10. **Privacy Officer designation** — required by some statutes

## Estimated review cost

A privacy + medical device attorney typically charges $300-700/hour. Initial review and revision of two documents like these typically runs 4-10 hours of attorney time, or $1,500-7,000. Additional revisions and ongoing compliance updates are recommended annually. Compared to potential statutory damages (BIPA: $1,000-5,000 per violation; CCPA: $2,500-7,500 per violation; FDA: significant), legal review is essential, not optional.
